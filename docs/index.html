        How it works

        Everything runs in your browser. The solver uses three ideas.

        1. Keep only the words that fit

        For every guess you enter, the solver asks each word in the list: "If this were the answer, would my guess have shown exactly these colours?" Only words that answer yes for every guess are kept. That is the possible count.

        This uses Wordle's own colouring rules, so double letters work: guess SPEED with one yellow E and one gray E and the answer has exactly one E.

        2. Put the common words first

        Wordle answers are everyday words, but the list of allowed guesses has almost 15,000 words, most of them obscure. So Likely answers sorts the possible words by how often they appear in English text. The top of the list is usually the answer.

        Hide past removes words that have already been the answer.

        3. Pick guesses that teach you the most

        Best start. Count how many common words (minus past answers) contain each letter. The ten most frequent letters are roughly S E A R I O L T N D. The solver looks for two real words with no letter in common that cover as many of those letters as possible, such as DEALS + INTRO. Two guesses then test ten different letters.

        After your first guess it does the same with letters you haven't tried yet, counted over the words that are still possible.

        Narrow it down. Sometimes you know four letters but many words still fit: BUDDY, MUDDY, CUDDY, … Guessing them one by one can lose the game. Instead the solver takes the 20 most likely answers and tries every allowed word as a guess, even ones that can't be the answer. Each guess splits those 20 words into groups by the colours they would show. The best guess leaves many small groups, so whatever colours you get, few words remain. A ★ means the guess could also be the answer.
